Working papers

Exact Parametric Causal Mediation Analysis with a Categorical Mediator with Santiago Montoya-Blandon
This paper develops a parametric framework for causal mediation analysis with nominal categorical mediators, such as occupation, education, or sector. Building on Imai et al. (2010), I derive closed-form expressions for the direct and indirect effects on the natural outcome scale, with the indirect effect decomposed additively by mediator category. The paper also clarifies the relationship with inverse odds ratio weighting, highlighting its strengths and limitations. Simulations show that the proposed approach performs well, especially when treatment-mediator interactions are present.
The Cost of a Gap Year: Education Delay and Career Outcomes with Anwen Zhang and Santiago Montoya-Blandon
This paper examines the association between taking a gap year before higher education and early-career earnings in the UK. Using the Next Steps cohort linked to administrative school records, I compare students who defer university entry with those who enrol immediately, using OLS, matching, and sensitivity analyses. Gap-year participation is associated with lower earnings at ages 25 and 32, though the penalty declines over time. The effects are largest for unplanned and non-labour-market gap years, while labour-market-oriented gap years show little effect. The findings highlight the importance of gap-year type for long-term outcomes.

Work in progress

When Shorter Lists Help: Constrained Sequential School Choice
Real-world school choice systems almost universally cap the number of schools students may rank, yet the theory of constrained school choice has been developed only for simultaneous, one-shot applications (Haeringer and Klijn, 2009). At the same time, a growing literature studies school choice as a sequential game in which students observe earlier applications before submitting their own (Dur, Hammond and Kesten, 2021), but without constraints. This paper combines the two: I study sequential school choice under list-length constraints, characterising equilibrium outcomes of the Boston mechanism, Deferred Acceptance, and Top Trading Cycles as the constraint varies. The interaction turns out to be far from neutral: the three mechanisms respond to the constraint in sharply different ways, and a binding constraint can act as a commitment device with real welfare consequences.
Safety in Numbers? Application Portfolios with Endogenous Cutoffs
Applicants hedge—a reach, a match, a safety. But a college is only a safety relative to where this year's admission cutoffs land, and those cutoffs are set by where everyone else applies. This paper studies application portfolios in a large market in which students know their own exam score but not the cutoffs, which fluctuate with cohort size and capacity. Because hedging is what determines cutoffs, safety is endogenous: insuring against a strong cohort raises the cutoffs at precisely the colleges applicants retreat toward. I characterise equilibrium portfolios and show that whether hedging creates value or merely wastes seats depends on whether declined offers are recycled.
Stable but Unreachable: Decision Risk in Decentralised College Admissions
In decentralised college admissions, students must commit to one offer before knowing how rivals will act or how they will be graded, and can therefore be wrong. We call this decision risk, and show it is identically zero under any strategy-proof centralised mechanism: the benchmark is a theorem, not an estimated counterfactual. Characterising the admissions game, we measure risk over stable matchings using confirmation weights—the chance a higher-ranked rival actually contests a seat. We characterise how risk varies across stable outcomes and with the information available to students, and show the measure is estimable from administrative data.
